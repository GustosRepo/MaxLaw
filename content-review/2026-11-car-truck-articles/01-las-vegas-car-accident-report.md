# How to Get a Las Vegas Car Accident Report

To get a Las Vegas car accident report, start with the agency that investigated the crash. Look at the officer’s card, incident paperwork or report number, then use that agency’s official records page. A Las Vegas mailing address alone does not tell you which department has the report.

If the other driver’s insurer asks for a report you don’t have yet, keep the incident number and ask which records it needs and where to send them. If you were injured, an attorney can help you understand how the report fits into your claim.

## Which agency has your crash report?

The Las Vegas Valley includes several police agencies with separate request systems. Use the agency name on your paperwork before paying for a search.

| Agency named on your paperwork | Where to start | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Las Vegas Metropolitan Police Department, often called LVMPD or Metro | [LVMPD report-copy instructions](https://www.lvmpd.com/about/bureaus/records-and-fingerprint-bureau/requesting-report-copies-with-lvmpd) | Request method, identification, authorization and case-release requirements |
| Nevada State Police Highway Patrol, often shown as NHP | [Highway Patrol crash-report requests](https://www.nhp.nv.gov/crashes-citations/crash-report-request/) | Crash number, involved person’s last name and crash date |
| Henderson Police Department | [Henderson Police records requests](https://www.cityofhenderson.com/government/departments/police/police-records/request-records) | Current report-request instructions and whether the report is available |
| North Las Vegas Police Department | [North Las Vegas Police records](https://www.cityofnorthlasvegas.com/residents/police/police-records) | The accident-report purchase link and current request requirements |

This matters whether the collision happened near the Strip, on I-15, in Henderson, in North Las Vegas or elsewhere in Clark County. Do not assume the nearest police station holds the record. If you have no paperwork, contact the records unit of the agency you believe responded and ask how to identify the report.

### LVMPD requests

LVMPD’s records guidance lists online, in-person and mail options. Have your event number, crash details and identification ready. Someone requesting a report for another person may need additional authorization. Follow the instructions for your role rather than assuming a family member can obtain every record.

Start on the department’s page and follow its request link. This helps you distinguish the agency’s process from unrelated services that charge to locate records.

### Highway Patrol requests

The Highway Patrol directs requesters to CRASHDOCS through its official page. Its instructions ask for the crash number, the last name of an involved person and the crash date. The crash number should include the “NHP” prefix.

At the time of this article’s source check, the agency listed a **$10 report fee** and said reports become available **7–14 days after the crash**. Check the current instructions before paying; those details do not establish another agency’s fees or guarantee that every report will be ready on that schedule. The Highway Patrol directs requests for fatal-crash reports and crash photographs to its regional offices.

### Henderson and North Las Vegas requests

For a Henderson investigation, begin with the city’s Police records page. For a North Las Vegas investigation, the city’s records page links to the accident-report purchase service, including LexisNexis BuyCrash. Use the links provided by the city rather than choosing a vendor from an advertisement.

Check the price, eligibility and release status in the relevant system. A fee listed for a general police report may differ from the amount charged through an accident-report service.

## What should you gather before making a request?

Put the information you already have in one place:

- The investigating agency and incident or crash number.
- The date, approximate time and exact location of the collision.
- The names of the people involved and vehicle plate information.
- Your identification and any authorization required for the request.
- Your preferred contact information and a copy of the request receipt.

These are preparation steps, not a claim that every agency requires exactly the same documents. If a report number is missing, ask the records unit what information it can use to locate the incident. Do not invent a number or submit repeated paid requests without checking.

Provide identification and sensitive documents only through the agency’s designated process. Keep copies of what you submitted and any response explaining why a record is unavailable.

## Is requesting a report the same as reporting the crash?

No. Requesting an existing police report, reporting a collision to authorities, filing a DMV form and notifying an insurer are separate tasks.

[NRS 484E.070](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-484E.html#NRS484ESec070) generally requires a driver’s DMV report within 10 days when a qualifying crash causes injury, death or apparent damage of at least $750 to a vehicle or item of property. The officer-investigation exception requires the officer’s report to contain specified insurance information. The statute also addresses incapacity and certain carrier situations.

The [Nevada DMV SR-1 form](https://dmv.nv.gov/pdfforms/sr1.pdf) provides instructions and attachment requirements. If no officer investigated, or you are unsure whether an exception applies, address that question promptly. Ordering records does not satisfy a separate reporting obligation or an insurance-policy notice requirement.

## What if the report is unavailable or contains a mistake?

Ask the records unit whether the report is still being processed, whether you used the right identifier, or whether your request needs additional documentation. A search that returns no result does not establish that no report exists.

When you receive it, check the names, vehicles, insurance details, location and descriptions against your own records. If you identify an apparent factual error, ask the investigating agency about its correction or supplemental-information process. Keep the original report and your supporting documents. Do not alter the report yourself.

A disagreement about how the crash occurred may require more than a records correction. Witness accounts, photographs and vehicle evidence can be important even when they do not appear in the report.

## What can a crash report establish?

A police report can help identify people, vehicles, observations and investigative leads. It does not replace a full examination of fault, injuries or insurance coverage. Whether a particular statement can be used in court is a separate legal question.

In a rear-end crash with three cars, check whether the report describes the order of impacts or simply lists the vehicles involved. If you remember two impacts, write down their order as you recall it and save any photos or video alongside the report. Those details can help an investigator work out whether your car struck the one ahead before or after it was hit from behind.

For someone returning home after visiting Las Vegas, keep the report and incident number with photos, treatment records and insurance correspondence. For a commercial truck crash, a public report may be only one part of the evidence; carrier logs, maintenance information and vehicle data may require separate steps.
