# What to Do After a Rear-End Collision in Las Vegas

After a rear-end crash, get to safety and seek medical care if you need it. Once you’re safe, save the incident number, any original photos or video, and the names of people who saw the crash. If an insurance adjuster calls, note who called and what they’re asking for. Those details give you something concrete to work with if the drivers disagree about what happened.

## Who is at fault when someone hits you from behind?

Nevada law requires drivers to leave a reasonable and prudent following distance for their speed, traffic, and road conditions. How that rule applies depends on what happened before the impact. A sudden lane change, brake-light problems, or an earlier impact in a chain reaction can change what the evidence shows. [Nevada’s following-distance rule, NRS 484B.127](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-484B.html#NRS484BSec127)

When Nevada’s comparative negligence rule applies, an injured person may recover if their share of fault is no greater than the combined fault of the defendants from whom they seek recovery. A qualifying award is reduced by the injured person’s share of fault. For example, a 20% share of fault reduces a qualifying award by 20%. An adjuster’s initial assessment is one part of the claims process. [Nevada’s comparative negligence statute, NRS 41.141](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-041.html#NRS041Sec141)

## What evidence is worth saving right away?

- **The order of events.** Write down what you remember while it’s fresh. If you felt two impacts, record which came first and where your vehicle was in relation to the others. Mark details you’re unsure about.
- **Photos and video.** If you can do so safely, save photos of the damage, lane markings, vehicle positions, and nearby signs. Export any dashcam clip before the device records over it and keep the original file. Write down where you noticed a camera and the time of the crash so someone can ask the operator whether footage exists.
- **The incident number.** Start with the agency listed on the officer’s paperwork. Use the official report request instructions if Las Vegas Metropolitan Police or Nevada State Police handled the crash. [LVMPD report copies](https://www.lvmpd.com/about/bureaus/records-and-fingerprint-bureau/requesting-report-copies-with-lvmpd) · [Nevada State Police crash reports](https://www.nhp.nv.gov/crashes-citations/crash-report-request/)

The crash report can help identify the vehicles and people involved. Photos, witness accounts, and video can fill in details about the sequence of impacts that the report leaves open.

## What should you ask when their insurer calls?

Write down the adjuster’s name, company, claim number, and callback number. Ask what they need, whether they’re asking for a recorded statement, and whether they can send the request in writing. Check your own policy for separate notice and cooperation requirements. If you receive a release, ask which claims it would resolve before signing it.

Keep the insurer’s letters with your treatment records, bills, and documentation of any missed work. If you’re unsure which records matter or what a release would cover, bring the paperwork you have to a consultation.
